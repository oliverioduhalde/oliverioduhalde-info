*Dandelion* is an automated sound sculpture made of wood, copper and electromechanical actuators. Its shape and sound are inspired in the dandelion seed — a symbol of life whose flying and hovering nature shows fragility and simpleness in life's propagation.

## Concept Stage

The formal departure point was the dandelion seed's morphology: its radial structure, suspended equilibrium, and capacity for dispersal. Equilibrium and final form were anticipated by 30cm wood models. First suspension attempts used high-performance fishing lines; later, a MIDI interface was attached to the boards, expanding Dandelion's expressive possibilities to live performance.

## Prototype Stage

Early prototypes addressed three main problems: form, balance and hovering methods, and electronics and electromechanics. Dandelion's generative music is inspired on bacterial exponential growth — a closed 12-semitone system represents 12 potential living bacteria. Different intervalic and spatial relationships can be triggered from a central matrix to build different growing patterns and consequent intervalic sound textures.

## Software

Dandelion's automatic sound generation is controlled by a generative algorithm developed in MAX/MSP. Its logic is inspired on bacterial exponential growth. A solid iron cast central piece forms the heart of Dandelion, integrating a reliable solenoid-based percussion mechanism within an inner skeleton structure of cubic tubes.

## Final Sculpture Development

The final object was developed in collaboration with Hugo Dominguez. Main structural challenges: central joint, mechanical transmission, and lever issues.

## Assembling & Exhibition

Assembled and exhibited at Espacio Fundación Telefónica, Buenos Aires, 2013.
